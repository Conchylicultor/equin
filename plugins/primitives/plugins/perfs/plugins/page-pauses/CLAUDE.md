# page-pauses

Was the page paused while something was being timed?

```ts
const p = pausesDuring(Date.now() - sample.totalMs, Date.now());
// Hidden time is throttled (it measures the browser); a stall is reported on
// its own. The two are disjoint, so they add up.
const ownMs = sample.totalMs - p.hiddenMs - p.stalledMs;
```

- **One owner.** Producers of client timings (live-state's `update`, the
  optimistic `confirm`, a resource's mount → settle) never track visibility.
  They emit a duration at its end, and the consumer places it on the wall clock
  and asks here. Before this, three producers each kept their own hidden flag,
  and the `element` settle kept none.
- **Hidden** covers `visibilitychange` → hidden and Page Lifecycle
  `freeze`, until visible / `resume` again. A page loaded hidden counts as
  hidden since `performance.timeOrigin`. `hiddenMs` is how much of the span
  was hidden (union), not whether: a slow op spanning a brief tab switch keeps
  its visible part. A stretch still in progress counts from its start, so a
  span that ended just before the tab was hidden stays visible even when it is
  asked about afterwards.
- **Stalled** is the union of long animation frames ≥ `STALL_MIN_MS`
  (1000 ms) inside the span, outside its hidden time — disjoint from
  `hiddenMs`, so a consumer subtracts both. That is the bar at which
  `reports/main-thread-stall` files a frozen main thread as its own `main-thread`
  slow-op (`STALL.SLOW_OP_MIN_MS`). The two must agree: a shorter frame is not
  reported anywhere, so it stays in the timed op. `longtask` is the fallback.
  Neither API means the answer is 0 (cannot tell).
- **Ask once the record is complete — `stallsKnownThrough(end)`.** A long
  frame's entry is delivered in a task of its own after the frame, and late on
  a page still busy from it: on main (2026-11-15) a 1102 ms frame opening a long
  page was still missing ≥ 380 ms after it ended, when the slow-op queue's
  fixed 250 ms flush judged the four updates and two settles behind it — six
  reports of one stall main-thread-stall had filed already. So the record says
  when it is complete: entries arrive in order, so once one starting at or
  after `end` has arrived, every frame that ended by `end` is in it (a busy
  page proves it within a frame or two); else after `STALL_ENTRY_GRACE_MS`
  (2 s, main-thread-stall's own wait for the same entry; one timer). The
  slow-op queue awaits it before judging; `hiddenMs` needs no wait.
- Rings are bounded at 100 intervals each. Installed on module load (event
  listeners and one `PerformanceObserver`; a timer only while a
  `stallsKnownThrough` waits).
