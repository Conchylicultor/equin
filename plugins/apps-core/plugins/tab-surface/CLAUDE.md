# tab-surface

The per-tab render core. `TabSurface` mounts one tab's `PaneSurfaceProvider`,
reports its leaf title to the tabs store, and renders the app — identically for
every placement, so docked / floating-window / solo differ only in the box they
hand it.

## An unloaded tab keeps its provider and its title

`TabSurface resident={false}` (the surface's residency policy, `tabs`'
`useResidentTabs`) renders only the tab's `PaneSurfaceProvider` and its title
reporter: the app, its sync-status and undo providers and its `mod+z` keys
unmount. The title reporter sits right inside the pane provider, not inside the
app, so an unloaded tab is still named in the tab bar. Mounted again, the app is
wrapped in `ScrollRevival` (auto-scroll), so its kept scrollers restore the
offsets they saved when it unloaded.

## The surface box is the overlay host

The tab's outer box is a `<SurfaceOverlayHost>` (`primitives/overlay/surface-overlay`),
not a plain `relative size-full` div. That box is the only element that IS the
surface, so it is where a pane's full-surface overlay has to land: a pane can
fill its own tab — tab bar and app rail still on screen and clickable — instead
of reaching for `<ViewportOverlay>`, which covers them. Because the host sits
inside whatever box the placement gave the tab, that works under all three
placements with nothing placement-specific written anywhere.

It stays `relative size-full` for its original reason too: the sync-status
indicator's `Pin` anchors to this surface's corner, and the app render fills it.

## The fallback body registers its placements

`AppTabsBody` (what `apps-core/layout` renders with no `Apps.Surface`, e.g. the
website) IS the surface then, so while mounted it registers docked-only
placement capabilities, like the surface body does. Without them the registry
stays empty and which app fills the surface is never known (`apps-core/theme-scope`).
