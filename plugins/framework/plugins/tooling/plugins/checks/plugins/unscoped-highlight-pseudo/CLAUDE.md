# unscoped-highlight-pseudo

Rejects a `::highlight(name)` rule in plugin CSS whose originating element is
any element — written with nothing before it (`::highlight(x)`, also after a
combinator or comma: `.a ::highlight(x)`) or after a bare `*`.

## The cost it prevents

Chrome computes the style of a matching highlight pseudo-element for every
element such a rule matches, on every style recalc, whether or not a highlight
of that name is registered. On a 17k-element agent-manager page (a 294-block
page open beside a conversation) one full recalc touched ~86k styles and took
390 ms; without the seven unscoped rules (the page editor's
`page-text-span`, the streaming tail's six `stream-tail-*` tiers) it took
111 ms, and scoped to their hosts 114 ms. Opening a long page pays two or three
full recalcs, so these rules were most of its 1.1 s frame.

## The fix it points to

Scope the rule to the element the highlight's ranges live in —
`[data-lexical-editor]::highlight(page-text-span)`, or a mark the painting
plugin owns (`steady-reveal`'s `data-stream-tail`, spread by its hook). The
text below it inherits the style through highlight inheritance (css-pseudo-4).
Avoid a descendant form (`:is(.host, .host *)::highlight(x)`): the ancestor
walk it costs on every element is most of what the unscoped rule cost.
