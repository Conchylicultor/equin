# heap-pacing

`pacedHeapEnv(env, ramBytes?)` returns a child's environment with
`BUN_JSC_forceRAMSize` set to `ramBytes` (default `HEAVY_PROCESS_RAM_BYTES`,
4 GB). Pass it as the `env` of a spawn that starts a heavy bun process — one
that holds GBs of live heap, such as a TypeScript program with typed ESLint, or
allocates GBs past a large live heap, such as a cold check pass.

## Why

JavaScriptCore collects when the heap has grown by a factor of the live size
that depends on the heap's size against the machine's RAM. After a full
collection with H bytes live, the heap may reach about
`(1 + heapGrowthMaxIncrease · e^(−heapGrowthSteepnessFactor · H / ram)) · H`.
Bun ships `heapGrowthMaxIncrease` 2 and steepness 2 (oven-sh/bun#39844). JSC
assumes the process has the whole machine to itself. On a 64 GB host a 4.4 GB
heap may therefore grow ~2.7× before a collection. Every heavy process does
this at once.

With `ram` = 4 GB:

- a heap far below 4 GB (a script, a keyed type-check run) still grows ~2–3×
  and collects as rarely as before;
- a heap of several GB grows ~1.1–1.3×.

The processes that hold the host's memory are the ones that pay for collecting
more often.

## Measured

The heaviest batch of a cold whole-tree type check: 2,000 sources, 4.4 GB live
after a full GC. Peak RSS went from 8.6–9.7 GB to 6.95–7.2 GB, CPU +0–10 %.
`--smol`, `Bun.gc(true)` at phase boundaries or between lint files, a lower
`heapGrowthMaxIncrease`, no concurrent GC and `MIMALLOC_PURGE_DELAY=0` each
moved the peak by less than run-to-run noise. See
`research/2026-11-08-global-type-check-bun-heap-pacing.md`.

## Not a cap

Bun has no heap limit: `--max-old-space-size` is ignored (oven-sh/bun#34917,
fix in review as oven-sh/bun#34924). This setting paces the collector; it
bounds nothing. A process whose live data exceeds 4 GB still runs, with a
growth factor near 1 (more frequent collections).

## Where it applies

- the type-check worker (`spawnTypeCheckWorker`, at its memory profile's pace:
  4 GB, or 1.5 GB on one host unit) and its batch processes, which run at
  their worker's pace (`parentPacedHeapEnv`);
- every check pass, at `CHECK_PROCESS_RAM_BYTES` (2 GB): a build's or push's
  check child (`runCheckSubprocess`), and a direct `./equin check` — the
  `./equin` shim exports the same value before it execs bun (JSC reads it once,
  at VM start), kept equal to the constant by this plugin's test;
- every `./equin run` script. A one-off script that builds a whole-repo
  program (an inventory, a rename) is the same workload; a small script is far
  below the pacing size and does not notice.

### The check pass

A check pass's own process holds ~1–1.5 GB live (every plugin barrel loaded, a
pass's memos). A cold pass — the first update after an install, before any
plugin record exists — allocates GBs of short-lived scan, fold and evaluation
work on top of it. Against an 8 GB machine, JSC let that heap reach ~2.4× its
live size before a full collection, so the cold pass peaked at 3.1–3.4 GB of
anonymous memory on an 8 GB Linux host: the process the kernel killed. Paced
at 2 GB: 2.3–2.6 GB, +10–15 % wall time
(`research/2026-11-19-global-cold-check-pass-gc-slack.md`).

## Whose pace a child runs at

A pace is a property of the process it was chosen for. `pacedHeapEnv` marks
the pace it writes (`EQUIN_HEAP_PACED`), and a child spawned with a pace of its
own replaces an inherited, marked one: a check pass paced at 2 GB still starts
its type-check worker at the worker's profile. A pace a person set (no mark)
wins everywhere below it, so a measurement can pick its own for a whole
process tree (`type-check/scripts/batch-memory-bench.ts`). A child that is its
parent's own work (a batch) keeps the parent's pace: `parentPacedHeapEnv`.
