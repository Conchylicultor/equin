# loop

Sonata A–B practice loop: a draggable loop region on the progression bar, a
Loop split button closing the player's transport strip (`SonataPlayer.Transport`,
after the scrubber), and `L`/`[`/`]` shortcuts that cycle playback within `[A, B]`.

The Loop control is a `ButtonGroup`: the main segment toggles the loop in one
click (as `L` does); the chevron opens a `ControlPanelPopover` with the loop's
repeat options, the `loopConfig` preferences (config_v2, both default ON,
rendered by the shared `FieldRenderer` switch rows):

- **Show loop ahead** (`showAhead`) — past B, the piano roll's falling notes
  show the loop repeating (`[rest][A, B)` again and again) instead of the song's
  continuation.
- **Rest before repeat** (`restBeforeRepeat`) — one empty bar (at A's meter)
  before every repeat, so the next pass can be seen coming.

They are preferences, not loop state: the headless `LoopOptionsSync`
(`Sonata.Effect`) pushes them into the session (`setLoopOptions`), which owns
what they mean — see the session's `loopCycle` and score/core's `LoopCycle` /
`unrollLoop`.

On the progression bar the loop is a rounded band over the rail zone, with an
accent pill handle on the rail at `A` and at `B` (`railBand(3)`); while enabled
the rail outside `[A, B]` is dimmed, and hovering the band reveals a clear
button above its top-right. Only the handles and the clear button stop the
pointer — a click inside the band still seeks.

The loop is also surfaced on the piano roll's falling-note timeline — a
scroll-synced `[A, B]` band with `A`/`B` boundary lines, contributed through the
generic `Sonata.TransportOverlay` slot so it scrolls glued to the notes. When
the roll unrolls the loop ahead (`projection.timeline.loop`), the band repeats on
every drawn copy within reach of the viewport (`loopOccurrences` — a fixed set:
the cursor never passes the first copy), each copy's band after its rest; the
cursor is placed and a scroll mapped back through `projection.timeline`. Each
boundary line (and its letter chip) is draggable there too, mapped back to a
beat through the projection's `yToBeat`; the rest of the band stays
pointer-transparent so dragging the lane still scrubs. Holding a dragged
boundary near the lane's top or bottom edge scrolls the roll, so a bound can be
carried past what is on screen: the roll scrolls by moving the playhead, so the
drag hands `useEdgeAutoScroll` a surface whose `scrollBy` seeks (playback pauses
while it scrolls and resumes on release).

Both surfaces snap a dragged bound with one rule (`snapToBars`): the nearest bar
line or the song's end — the end has to be a target of its own, since a song
that ends mid-bar has no bar line there. Holding Alt skips the snap.

Because the loop is usually taller than the lookahead, a boundary often sits
off-screen. The loop also surfaces a sticky off-screen `A`/`B` edge indicator —
a small arrow chip pinned to the lane's top/bottom edge (screen-anchored via the
new `Sonata.TransportEdge` slot, mounted outside the scroll layer) pointing
toward a boundary that has scrolled past the lookahead, complementing the
scroll-synced band. The content-space band label and the edge chip are mutually
exclusive: a single edge-bucket threshold (which also reserves the HUD's vertical
zone) decides per boundary whether its in-band label or its edge chip shows.

The loop transport state (`loop`/`setLoop`) and the wrap live in the `shell`
plugin alongside `isPlaying`/`tempoScale`; this plugin owns the whole UI.

**Seamless wrap (no audio gap):** the wrap is no longer an event-driven restart.
The transport anchors once and the rAF tick *computes* the looped position with a
deterministic time-fold (`positionAtTime` over the session's `loopCycle`, in
`score/core`); the audio engine's scheduler pre-schedules the same iterations end
to end from the same anchor + cycle (its cumulative-beat path), so the sound and
the cursor wrap together with no teardown — the rest before a repeat, when on, is
part of every iteration for all of them alike. The schedule is rebuilt only on a
deliberate change — toggling the loop, seeking, moving the bounds or switching
the rest — never on a plain wrap; tempo changes follow the existing loop-aware
`retime` path.
