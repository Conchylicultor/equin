What to focus on

    TimingA bar is 8 counts. Steps land on 1-2-3 and 5-6-7; 4 and 8 are pauses (optional tap). You start with the left foot on 1 and the right on 5. The position before count 1 is simply where count 8 of the previous figure left you.
    Step sizeCuban steps are small, about half a foot length. Weight on the balls of the feet, knees soft. The hip motion comes from bending and straightening the knees.
    ConnectionElbow bent, hands around belt height. Your hand moves because your body moves. Hooked fingers, never a grip, so her hand can swivel in turns.
    The beatThe campana marks every count (1 and 5 louder). The clave is the 2-3 son clave over the bar. The conga's open tones on 4 and 8 sit on the pauses: the moment to prepare.

    Cuban school versions; names and details vary between teachers and between Cuba and Miami rueda. Distances are to scale on a 50 cm grid but a little looser than real life, so the feet stay readable. If your teacher's version differs, trust the teacher.
